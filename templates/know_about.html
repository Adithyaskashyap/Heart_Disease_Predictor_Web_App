Know About Heart Health & AI Prediction

Understand the biological meaning behind every parameter used in the Heart Disease Prediction System.

What is Heart Disease?

The human heart continuously pumps oxygen-rich blood to all organs and tissues. Any abnormality affecting its structure, blood vessels, electrical system, or pumping ability can lead to heart disease — one of the leading causes of death worldwide.

Many heart conditions develop slowly and silently due to unhealthy lifestyle, genetics, stress, diabetes, obesity, smoking, or high cholesterol. Early prediction can significantly reduce the risk of:

  • Heart attack
  • Stroke
  • Cardiac arrest
  • Heart failure
  • Irregular heartbeat

Understanding Each Prediction Parameter

1

Age

Blood vessels lose elasticity with age and accumulate plaque. Risk increases sharply after 45, and again after 60.

2

Sex

Men are at higher risk of early heart disease. Estrogen offers women protection until menopause, after which risk rises.

3

Chest Pain Type

Typical Angina, Atypical Angina, Non-Anginal, or Asymptomatic — patterns help identify coronary artery disease.

4

Resting Blood Pressure

Force of blood on artery walls. Normal: 90/60–120/80 mmHg. High BP (>140/90) damages vessels and overworks the heart.

5

Cholesterol

Excess LDL forms plaques in arteries. Desirable: <200 mg/dL. High: >240 mg/dL increases heart-attack risk.

6

Fasting Blood Sugar

High fasting glucose may signal diabetes, which damages blood vessels and elevates cardiovascular risk.

7

Resting ECG

Measures the heart's electrical activity. Abnormalities indicate arrhythmia, enlargement, or past heart attacks.

8

Maximum Heart Rate

Highest BPM during exertion. Abnormal values may suggest poor fitness or reduced oxygen supply.

9

Exercise-Induced Angina

Chest pain during activity often indicates coronary blockage and elevated cardiovascular risk.

10

Oldpeak

ST-segment depression during exercise vs rest. Higher values suggest ischemia and coronary artery disease.

11

ST Slope

Upsloping → normal · Flat → possible abnormality · Downsloping → higher cardiac risk.
